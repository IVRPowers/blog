# The Branch With a Queue and the Branch Standing Empty Are the Same Network
---

An 8-branch network, one business day, 1,723 visits. 229 customers leave without being served: 13.3% of all visits. That same day, the in-person positions add up to 120 hours with nobody in front of them: 31% of installed capacity.

## A queue and an empty seat, at the same time, in the same network.

Neither number is unusual on its own. Together they say something no branch report shows: the network doesn't lack people, it can't move them. A free advisor in one branch can't serve the queue in the branch down the road. And each branch team is sized for a day that doesn't exist: the average one.

> Capacity is a property of the network, not of the branch.

## What happens when capacity is shared

The in-person positions stay where they are. What is added is a remote team of 8 agents shared by all 8 branches, reached from a Video Kiosk in the branch or from the customer's phone through a QR code. Same customers, same day:

| | In-person only | + Video Kiosks | + QR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customers lost | 229 · 13.3% | 76 · 4.4% | 55 · 3.2% |
| Average wait | 1.5 min | 0.6 min | 0.6 min |
| Cost per customer served | 13.50 USD | 12.64 USD | 12.58 USD |
| Network result for the day | 7,281 USD | 9,279 USD | 9,666 USD |

The shared team is busy 66% of the day. The cost per customer served goes down, even though more customers are served, because a remote interaction costs less than an in-person one.

## Dedicated or shared: the same headcount, two results

Take one busy branch inside that network, with 240 visits a day, Video Kiosks and QR.

- **One remote agent of its own:** the branch completes 91.7% of requested transactions, and the agent is busy 27% of the day.
- **The 8-agent team shared across the network:** that works out at 0.9 of an agent per branch on average, and the same branch completes 97.1%.

A dedicated agent waits for their own branch's peak. A shared team follows the peak from branch to branch. That is the logic of a video contact center as a service (VCCaaS): the unit of capacity is the network, not the site.

## Where this doesn't help

In a network whose branches all peak at the same hour: sharing works because peaks don't coincide. On anything that requires presence, such as cash, signatures and identity checks, which stays with the in-person positions. And where the remote agent lacks the same systems and authority to resolve as the branch, because then video only moves the queue somewhere else.

## How these figures were produced

A discrete-event simulation of one business day across 8 branches with 49 in-person positions. The cost per interaction uses the reference rates in our report *The CX Gap in Assisted Service*: 13.50 USD in person and 7.16 USD remote. The 8-minute abandonment threshold in the ticket queue comes from the same report. The rest are working assumptions that change with each network's data. The figures compare models; they are not a forecast.
