# Good for the Business, the Employee and the Customer: Breaking the Zero-Sum Game of Service
---

Customer service has three actors: 🏢 the business, 👩‍💻 the employee and 🙋 the customer.

For years, improving things for one of them meant that someone else paid the bill. Try it with any process and you'll see it almost always holds — even with AI or digital tools, someone loses:

* → If the business wanted to cut costs, it cut capacity or quality.
* → If it protected the employee, it lost availability or flexibility.
* → If it wanted to serve the customer better, structural costs went up.

**It was always a zero-sum game. Until we stopped thinking of service as a fixed, physical position.**

## The change is remote video

Video service separates two things that were always bound together: **where the customer is, and where the person serving them is.**

The agent can be in a contact center or in another branch, while the customer is in a branch, at a kiosk, in a store, on their phone, or anywhere else. In-person capacity is no longer anchored. Now it is shared — and that's where all three benefits appear.

* 1️⃣ **For the business** → more capacity and lower operating costs, with no extra floor space. The same team can serve different locations depending on where demand appears.
* 2️⃣ **For the employee** → steadier workload, better recognition and more flexibility. Cases reach whoever is available, and breaks can be covered by a remote colleague.
* 3️⃣ **For the customer** → less waiting, without losing human service. No travelling, nothing to learn, no queue to get a person in front of them.

## What it looks like in practice

In a reference deployment where customers could choose between in-person service and video service, these were the measured and compared results:

* ✅ **CSAT 9-10/10**, measured in real time after each video interaction
* ✅ **FCR 95%**, on a par with in-person service
* ✅ **Waiting time of 0-3 minutes**, compared with 20-25 minutes before
* ✅ **+50% operating capacity**
* ✅ **Fixed costs unchanged, variable costs down 50%**

These are real figures from one specific deployment, not a universal promise. But they show something that no other channel — digital or in person — had managed: all three actors gain at the same time.

> Video service doesn't remove the human. It removes the need for the human to be physically where the customer is.

And that changes the economics of direct service:

* ✅ Fewer idle, anchored positions and less lost working time.
* ✅ More shared capacity, without physical borders or local opening hours.
* ✅ Higher performance and better use of resources.
* ✅ Shorter waits for everyone.

Human service is where critical value is delivered. That is the real triple benefit: the business wins, the employee wins, the customer wins.

And that may be the biggest difference between digitalizing service coldly and redesigning it.
